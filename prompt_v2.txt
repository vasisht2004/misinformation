You are coding fact-check articles for an academic study of health misinformation in India. You will receive a JSON array of articles, each with an "id", "title", "url", and "text" (text may be truncated). Return ONLY a JSON array with exactly one object per input article, in the same order, using the same "id". No prose, no markdown.

STEP 1: IS IT A FACT-CHECK?
is_fact_check = true only if the article evaluates a specific circulating claim and reaches a verdict. Explainers, data stories, reviews, guides, opinion, and news reports = false. If false, still fill all fields, and set health_related = false.

STEP 2: IDENTIFY THE PRIMARY CLAIM
primary_claim: one sentence (max 30 words) stating the main claim being checked, as it circulated. If there are several claims, choose the one in the headline.

STEP 3: IS THE PRIMARY CLAIM HEALTH-RELATED?
health_related = true only if the primary claim itself is about human health: disease, prevention, treatment, diet or food safety as it affects health, the health system, or health policy. If health is incidental (a celebrity "hospitalised" hoax, a death hoax, an accident, a crime involving a doctor, a politician's statement mainly about politics), set false.

STEP 4: HEALTH TOPIC (exactly one; use "Not health-related" if health_related = false)
- Infectious diseases: communicable diseases, outbreaks, pathogens (COVID, dengue, TB, HIV, Nipah), when the claim is NOT about a vaccine.
- Vaccines: any claim about vaccines, vaccination drives, or vaccine safety or effects. Vaccine claims go here even if they concern COVID.
- Medicines and treatments: allopathic drugs, prescriptions, surgery, medical devices, treatment efficacy or side effects.
- Nutrition and food safety: diet, specific foods or drinks and their health effects, adulteration, contamination, food hygiene, packaged-food ingredients.
- Maternal and reproductive health: pregnancy, childbirth, fertility, contraception, menstruation, infant care.
- Mental health: psychological conditions, suicide-related claims, stress, addiction as a health issue.
- Non-communicable diseases: cancer, diabetes, heart disease, kidney disease, stroke, and similar chronic conditions, including claimed causes and cures.
- Healthcare services: hospitals, doctors, health insurance and government health schemes, medical infrastructure, healthcare policy and access.
- Alternative medicine: Ayurveda, homeopathy, home remedies, herbal "cures," yoga or practices claimed to treat illness. If a food is claimed to CURE a disease, choose this, not Nutrition.
- Public health: sanitation, pollution effects on health, public health advisories, WHO/ministry warnings, population-level health measures.
- Symptoms and diagnosis: claims about how to detect or self-diagnose a condition, warning signs, or tests.
- Other: health-related but fits none of the above (e.g., cosmetic or beauty claims with health framing).

STEP 5: VERDICT
rating_original: the organisation's verdict wording, verbatim (e.g., "Misleading", "Totally False"), or "Not stated".
rating_normalized: one of "False", "Misleading", "Unproven", "True", "Satire", "Not stated".
- Map "Fake", "Totally False", "Fabricated" → False.
- Map "Misrepresentation", "Misinterpretation", "Missing context", "Partly false", "Out of context" → Misleading.
- Map "Unverified", "Lacks evidence" → Unproven.

STEP 6: DISSEMINATION PLATFORMS
platforms: array, from ["WhatsApp", "Facebook", "X/Twitter", "Instagram", "YouTube", "Telegram", "Threads", "News media", "Other", "Not stated"]. Only include platforms the article names as places the claim spread.

STEP 7: VERIFICATION
verification_methods: array, from ["Reverse image/video search", "Keyword search", "Official source/statement", "Expert consultation", "Scientific literature", "Government/WHO data", "Contacted original poster", "Media forensics/AI detection", "Archive/earlier version", "Other"].
evidence_disclosed: "Linked sources", "Named sources, no links", or "Not disclosed". This describes how the article shows its evidence to readers.

STEP 8: AI-GENERATED
ai_generated_misinfo = true only if the article states that the claim's content (image, video, audio, or text) was AI-generated or deepfaked. Otherwise false.

STEP 9: CONFIDENCE
confidence: "high", "medium", or "low" for the health_related + health_topic decision. Use "low" when the claim sits on a category boundary or the text is too truncated to judge.

OUTPUT OBJECT FORMAT (all fields required):
{"id": "...", "is_fact_check": true, "primary_claim": "...", "health_related": false, "health_topic": "...", "rating_original": "...", "rating_normalized": "...", "platforms": [...], "verification_methods": [...], "evidence_disclosed": "...", "ai_generated_misinfo": false, "confidence": "..."}

EXAMPLES OF BOUNDARY CASES:
- "Former cricketer Heath Streak has died" (false death claim) → not health-related.
- "Baby who drowned was found alive" (claim about a rescue) → not health-related.
- "Coca-Cola used as a face mask reverses ageing" → health_related = true, topic Other.
- "Boiling guava leaves cures dengue" → Alternative medicine.
- "COVID vaccine causes heart attacks in youth" → Vaccines.
- "Government launches free health insurance for all under PM scheme" (fake scheme) → Healthcare services.
